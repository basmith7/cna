# Bombing

Any aircraft with a bomb load above zero may bomb. Bombing missions are
named by their target. Some bombers can also bomb by night, safer from
enemy fighters but less accurate, and some aircraft may strafe while they
bomb (39.2). The result of a raid is read from the bomb points delivered on
the Air Bombardment Table, in the part for that kind of target.

::: spi 41.0

**Planning.** Once a Game-Turn, during strategic air planning, each
player assigns, in writing, the bombers he means to use that turn either to
**land support** in general (not yet to particular missions) or to
**strategic** missions: raids on Malta (Axis), convoy reconnaissance and
bombing (Commonwealth). Malta raids are resolved at once, in that stage;
convoy missions in their segments of the convoy stage. Either way those
bombers are done until the next Game-Turn. In each of the turn's three land
support air phases, bombers assigned to land support are given particular
missions, and the missions are resolved after air-to-air combat and flak.

::: spi-omit 41.1 41.2 41.3 41.4 41.6 41.7 41.8 41.9 — subsection headings; the rules under them are restated below

## Restrictions on bombers

::: spi 41.11 41.12 41.13 41.14

"Bombers" here means aircraft flown without a pilot. They never scramble and
have no air ZOC. A bomber bombs one target per mission, chosen before it
takes off: nothing is dropped on another target on the way home. A bomber
may abort before bombing (39.3), but its bombs count as dropped somewhere
and its ammunition is spent. To fly anything but a transfer it must be
fuelled, refitted and armed.

::: spi 41.15 41.16 41.17

Bombers' TacAir ratings are in parentheses: they only return fire at
fighters attacking them (§45). Aircraft with **D** capability may strafe
and bomb one target together (dive bombers, and fighters that carry bombs),
but a fighter-bomber flies either a bombing or a fighter mission, never
both; on a bombing mission its TacAir counts as parenthesised unless it
jettisons its bombs. Torpedoes have two targets only: ships and ports.

## Land support bombing

::: spi 41.21

Land support bombing hits installations, ports, troops and equipment to help
the war on the ground. When missions are assigned in a land support air
phase, each bomber assigned to land support for the turn gets a mission
and a hex, for instance `B-PF/Bardia` for Wellingtons sent against Bardia's
port. One counter per aircraft type per mission goes on the target hex, the
sheet recording which aircraft it stands for, unless enemy CAP intercepts
on the way (40.27): watch the path of flight through enemy offensive CAP
and its air ZOC. Scrambling fighters may also intercept.

::: spi 41.22

Over the target: air-to-air combat, then flak, then the survivors bomb. The
defender totals the bomb points delivered on that target, rolls two dice in
order on that target's part of the
[Air Bombardment Table](#air-bombardment-table), and applies the result.
Survivors fly home, still open to interception by enemy CAP.

### Targets

Every land support target except mined harbours is resolved on the Air
Bombardment Table.

::: spi 41.31

**Troops (B-CU).** The result is the number of battalion-equivalents in the
hex **pinned** ([below](#pinned-by-bombing)); for this, a tank or gun unit
(artillery, anti-tank) counts as two, and AA units may be pinned the same
way. The defender chooses which units as for an artillery barrage (12.2).
Units in a major city cannot be bombed until its fortification level is
down to 1 or less.

::: spi 41.32

**Trucks in convoy (B-TC).** The result is truck points destroyed, spread as
evenly as possible over truck types and cargoes. Trucks in a major city
cannot be bombed until the city's level is 0. A unit's attached first-line
trucks may be bombed the same way, but only by aircraft with **D**
capability.

::: spi 41.33

**Flak destruction (B-FS).** The result is AA-rated TOE strength points
destroyed; AA may also be pinned as above. Unlike fighter flak suppression,
it comes after flak has fired.

::: spi 41.34

**Ships (B-CF).** The result is damage points on the ships in the hex
(30.34). The Commonwealth fleet is never bombed at night. Ships may also be
torpedoed (41.6, 41.7). Axis coastal shipping cannot be bombed.

::: spi 41.35

**Supply dumps (B-SD).** The result is how much of every kind of supply in
the dump is destroyed, as a percentage. Unattached trucks in the hex also lose one truck
point per full 10 % destroyed, chosen by the defender and spread evenly.

::: spi 41.36

**Air facilities (B-AF).** The result is capacity levels lost. For each
level, 10 % of the aircraft on the ground there are destroyed, rounded down.

::: spi 41.37

**Fortifications and major cities (B-F/C).** Any major city or fortified
hex may be bombed; a major city by night too. At most one fortification
level falls per Operations Stage (see also 22.35). The same mission may
silence a temporary repair facility outside a major city: a result that
would cut a fortification level puts it out of use for one Operations Stage.

::: spi 41.38

**Roads and railways (B-R, B-RR)**, resolved like cities on their own rows:
the result is the hex's road or railway destroyed. A road may be bombed by
night only in a coastal hex. (Railways are quickly mended; whether cutting
them pays is the bomber's affair.)

::: spi 41.39

**Mining harbours (B-MH).** For each six aircraft laying mines, roll one die
on the Mining Harbor by Plane Table, entering by the average bomb load of
the six. At or under the number, one mine counter goes in the port hex; a
mine has the effect of blocking the harbour. **Ports (B-P)** lose
efficiency (55.1): total the bomb points and any strafing (40.66) delivered,
and the Air Bombardment Table gives the efficiency levels lost (Tripoli and
the other boxes, 41.8). Ports may also be attacked with torpedoes.

## Night bombing

::: spi 41.41 41.42 41.43

Bombers with capability **N** (or the Commonwealth's restricted **0**) may
bomb by night, trading accuracy for safety. Only night fighters may escort
them. A night mission is marked as such, for instance `B-P(N)`; the
[mission summary](40-missions.md#mission-summary) says which missions may
be flown at night.

::: spi 41.44 41.45

Day missions are all resolved before night ones, and the defender is told
which missions are night missions before anything (scramble included) is
resolved. Night bombers' bomb points are **halved**, rounded down, and
torpedoes stay home at night.

::: spi 41.46 41.47

Against night bombers, scramble is for night fighters alone (40.9). Other
fighters may fly offensive CAP against a night raid, with no air ZOC, and
only against enemy missions in their own hex. Every squadron of fighters,
CAP or scrambling night fighters, must first **find** the bombers: one die
per squadron, found on a **1**, with **2 subtracted** from the roll for a
squadron of night fighters. Found, the bombers and any night escort are
fought normally; not found, the fighters go home.

::: spi 41.48 41.49

Flak against a night mission fires **one column left**; already in the
first column, it has no effect. Night bombers get no formation-flying bonus
(45.36).

## Air Bombardment Table {#air-bombardment-table}

::: spi 41.5 41.51

The Air Bombardment and Secondary Barrage Targets Table is data:
[`data/tables/air-bombardment.json`](https://github.com/basmith7/cna/blob/main/data/tables/air-bombardment.json).
Eleven columns, entered by bomb points (1–20 up to 471+), torpedo points
(1–5 up to 341+) or barrage points; seven target groups, each read on two
dice in order:

| Group | Result |
|---|---|
| Airfields, landing strips, ports | levels lost, 0–4 |
| Supply dump | 0 % to 75 % of the supplies |
| Fortification | no effect or one level less |
| Railway; road | no effect or destroyed |
| Trucks, flak, combat units, Commonwealth fleet | 0–7: truck points, AA points, units pinned or damage points |
| Axis naval convoy | 0 % to 50 % of the cargo |

::: spi 41.52

The Mining Harbor by Plane Table is data too:
[`data/tables/harbour-mining.json`](https://github.com/basmith7/cna/blob/main/data/tables/harbour-mining.json)
(average bomb load 0–5: no chance; each further 5 points, one more pip;
31 or more: 6).

## Bombing Axis convoys

::: spi 41.61 41.62 41.63

Axis convoys sail the convoy lanes (55.1); the Axis Naval Convoy Air
Distance Chart (56.1) gives each lane's distance from certain key cities.
Commonwealth bombers on strategic missions, from Africa or Malta, may bomb a
convoy within their range; from Africa, the distance is to one of the lane's
listed cities plus on from there to the lane. Axis fighters based in North
Africa never cover convoys; those based around the Mediterranean may cover
any lane in range. The Commonwealth may send fighters on CAP or flak
suppression over any lane in range, measured the same way.

::: spi 41.64 41.65

Only convoys **found** by convoy reconnaissance (42.5) may be attacked. The
procedure:

1. Each side splits its aircraft between land support and strategic
   missions; the Axis splits its strategic aircraft between Malta raids and
   convoy cover.
2. The Commonwealth flies convoy reconnaissance (immune to fighters and
   flak).
3. The Axis puts its cover on CAP over particular lanes; the Commonwealth
   sends CAP, flak suppression and bombers to lanes where a convoy was
   found. If none was found, its aircraft stay on the ground and need no
   readying.
4. Air-to-air combat in each lane.
5. Flak suppression over the convoy.
6. The convoy's flak fires.
7. The Commonwealth bombs. Survivors return in the strategic air recovery
   stage.

::: spi 41.66 41.67 41.68

Total the bomb points (adjusted for torpedoes, 41.73) and roll two dice on
the convoy rows of the Air Bombardment Table: the result is the percentage
of the cargo lost. The loss is taken evenly, by percentage, from every kind
of cargo, fractions **rounded up**. For this, all truck replacement points
are one kind of cargo, as are all Italian tanks, all German tanks, all
Italian guns and all German guns. Convoy bombing never delays scheduled
reinforcements.

## Torpedoes

::: spi 41.71 41.75

Aircraft with **T** capability may carry torpedoes (arming them, 38.47),
which work against ships and ports.

::: spi 41.72

Axis torpedo bombers against the Commonwealth fleet use the **torpedo**
heading of the Air Bombardment Table, not the bomb heading; a raid mixing
the two rolls twice, once for the bombs and once for the torpedoes.

::: spi 41.73 41.74

Against an Axis convoy, if **at least half** the Commonwealth bombers carry
torpedoes, the bomb points are raised by **a quarter**, rounded up: 120
points from 24 aircraft, 12 with torpedoes, count as 150. Against ports,
torpedoes count as ordinary bombs.

## Bombing Tripoli

::: spi 41.81 41.82 41.83

Commonwealth bombers may raid the Tripoli and Tunisia boxes, but only to cut
port capacity or destroy aircraft on the ground. The Air Distance Chart
gives the distance from map hexes to each box; add it to the distance flown
on the map. A raid on the port of Tripoli or of Bizerta is an ordinary port
bombing (41.39).

::: spi 41.84 41.85 41.86

A raid on grounded aircraft there is flown as air facility bombing, but
only the aircraft losses apply, not the facility's. Each box's flak is
printed in it, fixed and indestructible. Axis CAP over a box covers every
target in it. The port of Tripoli may be bombed by night.

## Pinned by bombing {#pinned-by-bombing}

::: spi 41.9

A pin from bombing is not a barrage pin; this section replaces the looser
definition of *pinned* in 3.1 for bombing. What it does depends on whether
the unit's owner is the A or the B player of the Operations Stage.

::: spi 41.91

**A player's unit**, for the rest of the stage:

- it may voluntarily spend only **half its CPA**, rounded down;
- it may not voluntarily enter an enemy ZOC, nor leave one it is in;
- it may not start combat: no barrage, no offensive close assault, no
  anti-armour fire.

The pin ends when the stage passes to the B player.

::: spi 41.92

**B player's unit**: during the A player's part of the stage it may neither
move nor fight voluntarily; in the B player's part, the A player's
restrictions above apply. The pin ends with that Operations Stage.
