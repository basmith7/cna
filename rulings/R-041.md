# R-041 — Part-blocks of 5 CP: per move or across the stage?

## Problem

Case 49.13 charges the consumption rate for every five CP, "or fraction
thereof", spent moving. Case 49.16 has fuel drawn at the start of each
Movement Segment for the distance about to be moved, and 49.15 has players
track movement since the last refill. It does not say whether the
part-block is rounded up each time fuel is drawn, or whether CP are summed
over the stage (or since the last refill) and rounded up once. A unit that
moves 3 CP in each of three segments pays three blocks under the first
reading and two under the second.

## Options

1. **Round up each draw.** Every Movement Segment's movement is its own
   count of five-CP blocks. Consequence: simple and local to the segment;
   moving in short hops costs noticeably more fuel.
2. **Running total per Operations Stage.** Sum movement CP over the stage
   and charge whole blocks as they are started; a part-block paid in one
   segment covers the rest of that block in a later one. Consequence:
   fairer to short moves; requires carrying a CP remainder per Strength
   Point within the stage.
3. **Running total since last refill.** As option 2, but the remainder
   carries until the unit next fills up. Consequence: closest to "record how
   far each unit has moved since it was replenished" in 49.15; the most
   bookkeeping.

## Decision

Option 1. Each Movement Segment's movement is priced on its own: its CP are counted in blocks of five, a part-block counting as a whole one.

## Rationale

The printed procedure settles it. Case 49.16 has the player note how far
the unit will go *in that Movement Segment*, subtract that much fuel from a
source in the starting hex, and then move; a later move in the same stage
draws afresh. The fuel is paid before the move and only for that move, so
the "fraction thereof" of 49.13 can only be rounded at that point. A
running remainder (options 2 and 3) would need a record the rules never
ask for; 49.15's record of distance since the last refill exists to know
how much is left in the tanks, not to carry part-blocks forward. The cost
of short hops is real but is a plain consequence of the text rather than
an exploit, and it is the simplest rule for an engine: one rounding per
draw, no state carried between segments.
