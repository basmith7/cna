# R-010 — Retreat before assault: any direction, or away from the enemy?

## Problem

A retreat forced by the Close Assault CRT (15.82) has a direction: the units
must finish a set number of hexes further from the enemy that beat them, by
the easiest route toward friendly supply or a city. Retreat before assault
(13.2x) is a voluntary move made before any assault is resolved, and 13.27
says only that, being voluntary, it is handled "somewhat differently" from
the mandated kind. It does not say which of 15.82's constraints, if any,
carry over. NJHarman:[^nj-rba]

> Retreat before Assault does not follow the requirements of involuntary
> retreat [15.82]. Retreat Before Assault may be an "advance". *My reading of
> [13.27]' voluntary*

[^nj-rba]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Retreat Before
  Assault, <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#retreat-before-assault>,
  CC-BY-SA 4.0 (tagged INTERPRETATION). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **Ordinary movement in any direction** (NJHarman). The unit pays CP and
   the break-off cost, obeys the 13.2x limits (no entering an enemy ZOC, the
   4-CP/one-hex cap when not adjacent), and may otherwise go where it
   likes — including sideways or forward past the threatening enemy.
   Consequence: retreat before assault is a repositioning tool as much as an
   escape; the restated rule reads it this way.
2. **Must move away from the enemy.** The unit must end its move further
   from the nearest enemy combat unit than it started, as a mandated retreat
   does, but without the toward-supply and hexes-not-retreated penalties.
   Consequence: closer to the word "retreat"; but no case supplies the
   constraint, and the ZOC ban already stops a unit from advancing into
   contact.

## Decision

Option 1. Retreat before assault is ordinary voluntary movement under the 13.2x limits and has no required direction.

## Rationale

Option 1. 13.27 distinguishes the two kinds of retreat rather than tying
them together, and the 13.2x cases state their own limits in full (cohesion
floor, ZOC ban, CP cap, dump-blowing exception) without referring to 15.82.
Where SPI wanted a directional constraint it wrote one, as in 15.82 itself.
The restated rule already treats the move as voluntary movement under those
limits ([Combat — Retreat before assault](../rules/60-combat.md#retreat-before-assault)).
No engine probe covers this ruling. Option 2 would add a constraint no case
states, and an engine already enforces the 13.2x limits without it.

Decided under Brian's delegation of 2026-09-26; open to dispute like any
ruling (see [rulings/README.md](README.md)).
