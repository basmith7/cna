# R-142 — Which organisation-chart slot each counter fills

## Problem

The Formation Organisation Charts (19.3) draw each formation as symbols:
an infantry battalion, an artillery unit, "any three 1 SP artillery
units", "up to three anti-tank companies". The O/A sheets list the actual
counters. Nothing links one to the other, so an engine judging a shell
(9.26, [R-141](R-141.md)) cannot tell which slot a counter stands in.
cna-engine hit three cases: the 15th Panzer Division's artillery group is
three artillery counters, not one; the Polish brigade's light
anti-aircraft or machine-gun slot is a battalion, but the sheet assigns it
a machine-gun company; the 1st Free French Brigade has five infantry
battalions for three infantry slots.

## Options

1. **By stacking size alone.** Any battalion-equivalent fills any 1 SP slot,
   any brigade-equivalent any 2 SP slot. Simple, but five Free French
   battalions would then fill all five of their brigade's counted slots.
2. **By kind and size, from the characteristics chart, with the chart's
   multi-counter slots kept.** Each counter fills the slots whose symbol
   matches its kind (its 4.46 row: infantry, artillery, anti-tank,
   anti-aircraft, reconnaissance, engineer, tank) and its size (a
   battalion slot takes a battalion-equivalent, a company slot a company).
   Where the chart's key lets several counters fill one slot, they fill it
   together. Each counter fills at most one slot at a time.
3. **By the O/A sheet.** Whatever the sheet assigns fills a slot, in sheet
   order. This reproduces the history, but leaves no rule for a unit
   reassigned in play (19.2).

## Decision

Option 2. Each counter in `data/oa/` lists in `fills` the slots it fills
alone and in `fills_part` those it fills with others of its kind, and
`part_slots` in `data/tables/formation-organisation.json` says how many.

## Rationale

The chart's key names its symbols by kind ("anti-tank regiment", "light
anti-aircraft unit") and prints a size beside each, so both have to match;
option 1 drops the kind and lets surplus battalions stand in for artillery.
Option 3 follows the sheets but gives nothing for a counter reassigned
later, which 19.2 allows. Option 2 is read straight off the two charts.

How the counters were matched:

- **Kind** comes from the counter's 4.46 row. Commonwealth reconnaissance
  counters fill the armoured car or the armoured recce slot according to
  their `vehicle` (armoured-car or armoured-recce). Italian RECo groups are
  armoured car regiments; their other armoured car counters are battalions.
  Italian artillery counters named regiments fill the artillery regiment
  slot alone, and artillery battalions are one of up to three.
- **"X or Y" in the key** is kept: a German anti-tank battalion may fill a
  tank-destroyer slot (not the reverse); an Italian Bersaglieri regiment is
  a motorised infantry regiment, so it fills an infantry regiment slot as
  well; the 288th Sonderverband and the 361st Afrika Regiment fill any
  German infantry regiment slot; the 2/1 Australian Pioneers fill an
  infantry or the engineer slot.
- **Multi-counter slots:** the German artillery group is one artillery
  regiment HQ (not the ArKo) with its units, or three 1 SP artillery
  counters, all three needed; the Italian artillery regiment is a regiment
  counter, or one to three artillery battalions; the Italian anti-tank
  battalion is a battalion, or one to three anti-tank companies.
- **Commonwealth "any unit"** is filled by any battalion or company counter
  other than a tank battalion, as its key says.
- **HQs** fill the slot of their own `organisation`. The Libyan Tank
  Command's two slots are the 3 SP tank regiment, as their printed 3
  shows, and now name `tank-regiment-3sp`.

The three cases cna-engine raised:

- The 15th Panzer's 33rd and 140th Artillery Regiments and the I/115
  Artillery Battalion are its three 1 SP artillery counters: together they
  fill the artillery group, which counts as one brigade-equivalent.
- The Kopanski MG Company is a company. It fills the Polish brigade's
  unfigured "any unit" slot, not the 1 SP battalion slot. The brigade can
  fill at most four of its five counted slots, so it stops being a shell
  once it has four, and is one with three or fewer (two-thirds of five).
- Only three of the 1st Free French Brigade's five infantry battalions fill
  slots. The other two are the extra units the O/A sheet assigns beyond the
  chart (19.2). They do not count toward the share, so five battalions
  alone fill three of five counted slots and the brigade is a shell.

An engine asks which slots a formation's attached counters fill; any
assignment that fills the most counted slots is the one to use. A counter
the O/A sheet assigns that fits no slot of its parent (the Commonwealth
light anti-aircraft regiments of the infantry divisions, the Italian
anti-tank companies of the semi-motorised divisions) stays assigned under
its O/A notes but fills no slot.
