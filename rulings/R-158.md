# R-158 — Fortifications in "the hexes adjacent to" Tobruk

## Problem

63.75 puts a level 1 fortification in each of "the four hexes adjacent to
Tobruk". On the map Tobruk (C4807) has six neighbours, but three are sea
(C4808, C4907, C4908); only C4707, C4708 and C4806 are land. Operation
Crusader (62.5) fills "all hexes adjacent" to Tobruk and Bardia without a
number. An engine has to know how many fortifications to place and where.

## Options

1. **The land hexes only.** "Adjacent to Tobruk" means its land
   neighbours: three round Tobruk, one level each; 63.75's "four" places
   nothing more. In Crusader the same reading gives three round Tobruk and
   four round Bardia.
2. **Three adjacent plus Tobruk's own hex.** The fourth level goes in
   C4807 itself.
3. **The owner places the fourth** in any one of the three land hexes,
   raising it to level 2.

## Decision

Option 1. A set-up fortification row fills each land hex next to its place
once: three round Tobruk (C4707, C4708, C4806) and four round Bardia
(C4220, C4221, C4320, C4420), in both scenarios.

## Rationale

The printed text says "adjacent", and a fortification cannot stand in a sea
hex, so the map's three land neighbours are what the text can mean; "four"
is a miscount (Bardia, in the same case, does have four land neighbours).
Option 2 puts a fortification in a hex the text does not name, and option 3
creates a level 2 the scenario never prints. Option 1 matches Crusader's
"all hexes adjacent", which needs no number at all, and is the simplest to
enforce: every row's count now equals the place's land neighbours, which is
what cna-engine already places (#132).

supersession like any ruling (see [rulings/README.md](README.md)).
