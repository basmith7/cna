# R-109 — Open points in the campaign game's victory rules

## Problem

- The Axis automatic win needs Alexandria and Cairo held "for one full
  Game-Turn"; it does not say whether that means a Game-Turn completed
  from its first stage, or any three consecutive stages.
- The Axis win allows a 90-point supply line, the Commonwealth's test a
  60-point one. Deliberate or a slip?
- The three-point cap on withdrawal points may be per withdrawal or for
  the unit's whole game.
- The ratio of totals is undefined when the smaller total is zero or
  negative (returning withdrawn units costs points).

## Options

1. **Literal and simple.** A calendar Game-Turn; both line lengths as
   printed; the cap is per unit for the game; a positive total against
   zero or less is a smashing victory, both at zero or less a draw.
2. **Generous to play.** Any three consecutive stages; 90 for both tests;
   the cap per withdrawal; as option 1 for the ratio.

## Decision

Option 1. "One full Game-Turn" is a Game-Turn held from its first OpStage to its last; the 90- and 60-point lines stand as printed; the three-point withdrawal cap is per unit for the whole game; a positive total against zero or less is a smashing victory, and both at zero or less is a draw.

## Rationale

Each part is the plainer reading of the printed words. A "full Game-Turn"
is a unit of the turn sequence, not any three stages, and the Axis must
already hold every hex of both cities, so the stricter timing costs little.
The two line lengths test different things (an Axis that can win at once,
and an Axis that has run out of supply entirely), and nothing marks either
as a slip; the longer line for the Axis win is the generous one, which suits
a test that ends the game. A cap per withdrawal (option 2) would make the
cap meaningless for a unit withdrawn, returned and withdrawn again six
months later. A ratio has no value when the smaller total is zero or less,
and treating any positive total against it as the largest gap is the only
reading that keeps the ladder in order.
