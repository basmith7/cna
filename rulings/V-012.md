# V-012 — Interception by range roll; DCAP screens only as escort

## What the printed rules say

Combat air patrols intercept through the air zone of control, and screening follows 45.32.

## The change

NJHarman's house rule:[^nj]

> Interception and scramble are by squadron.
> In place of Air ZOC, OCAP squadrons not already in a hex with enemy aircraft
> must choose one hex containing enemy aircraft within six hexes and attempt to
> intercept them by rolling d6 greater than or equal the range to target hex.
> +1drm if target hex is less than or equal to ½ OCAP’s range from OCAP’s base.
> Each squadron may make only one interception roll.
> Scrambled aircraft are considered to be on OCAP. They may not intercept again.

> Only fighters on DCAP count for screen [45.32].
> All fighters on DCAP in hex are grouped together and screen all non-CAP
> aircraft in hex. Even if those aircraft “flew into” hex without an escort.
> Fighters on missions other than CAP are screened.
> DCAP is only for escorting other aircraft. Fly OCAP to “defend” a facility,
> fleet, etc. Fighters on other missions, Strafing for example, are too busy
> doing that to defend bombers.

[^nj]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Air War, OCAP and DCAP,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#ocap>, CC-BY-SA 4.0 (tagged CHANGE ADDITION, for the whole Air War section). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Effect on play

Each OCAP squadron picks one enemy-occupied hex within six hexes and must roll at least the range to intercept, with a bonus close to its base; scrambled aircraft count as OCAP. Only DCAP fighters screen, and they screen every non-CAP aircraft in the hex.

## Why it is recorded as a variant

The air zone of control and the printed screening rule are replaced.
