# R-043 — Fuel capacity when the CPA is not a multiple of five

## Problem

Case 49.14 sets capacity = CPA × 1/5 × consumption rate and says this is
always exactly enough to spend the whole CPA moving. Case 49.13 charges a
full block for any part of five CP. For a CPA that is not a multiple of
five the two disagree: a CPA of 22 with rate 4 gives a capacity of 17.6,
but spending all 22 CP costs five blocks, 20 Fuel Points.

## Options

1. **Round the CPA up to a multiple of five** (capacity = blocks × rate:
   20 in the example). Consequence: keeps the "exactly sufficient" promise
   and whole Fuel Points.
2. **Use the formula and round down** (17 in the example). Consequence:
   the unit cannot spend its full CPA on one tank, contradicting 49.14's
   note.
3. **Use the capacity printed on the organisation sheets as-is**, and apply
   this ruling only where no value is printed. Consequence: defers to the
   per-unit data, which may already embody one of the above.

## Decision

Option 1. Capacity is the CPA rounded up to a whole number of five-CP blocks, times the consumption rate.

## Rationale

Case 49.14 gives both a formula and the purpose of the formula: a full
tank is always exactly enough to spend the whole CPA moving. For a CPA
that is a multiple of five the two agree; for any other CPA only option 1
keeps the stated purpose, because 49.13 charges a whole block for the last
part-block of movement ([R-041](R-041.md) rounds that part-block up each
segment). Option 2 would strand every such unit short of its CPA on a full
tank, which the case says never happens. Option 3 does not answer the
question: where the organisation sheets print a capacity, that printed
value is per-unit data and governs as printed; this ruling applies when a
capacity must be derived, which is what an engine needs. Whole blocks keep
Fuel Points whole, the simplest reading to implement.
