# R-023 — Close assault, defender +2 column: readings 34–36

## Problem

On the Close Assault Results Table (15.79) the defender's +2 column prints
its 10 % cell as 24–33 and its 5 % cell as 41–52, so readings 34, 35 and 36
inflict no printed result. Both scans agree and the September 1979 errata is
silent. The gap is declared in `known_gaps` of
`data/tables/close-assault-results.json`. The cna-engine probe finds that
8.3 % of the rolls in this column read 34–36, so it comes up in play.

## Options

1. **The row above: 10 %.** Treat the 10 % cell as 24–36. Consequence: the
   defender loses a little more at +2; the 10 % cell becomes the widest in
   the column.
2. **The row below: 5 %.** Treat the 5 % cell as 34–52. Consequence: the
   cell starts where the +1 column's 5 % cell starts (34), and the 10 % cell
   keeps its printed end, which is also +1's (33).
3. **No loss.** Readings 34–36 inflict nothing. Consequence: the defender
   loses less at +2 than at +1 on those readings (+1 gives 5 %), so the
   column would reward a worse differential.

## Decision

Option 2. At +2, defender readings 34–36 inflict a 5 % loss (the 5 % cell reads 34–52).

## Rationale

No printed text settles it, so the choice rests on the table's own shape.
Loss must not fall as the differential rises: at +1 these readings give 5 %
and at +3 they give 10 %, so either option 1 or 2 keeps the column
monotone and option 3 does not. Of the two, the 10 % cell's printed end
(33) is identical to the +1 column's, while the 5 % cell's printed start
(41) is the odd number out: +1 starts it at 34 and +3 at 43. Reading the
misprint as the 5 % cell's start changes only the bound that looks wrong,
and fills the gap with the smaller of the two neighbouring losses. The
probe ([`probes/chart-oddities.json`](https://github.com/basmith7/cna-engine/blob/15a12384f3bd7aca3f815ad4f39ef8d5a52ecca2/probes/chart-oddities.json))
shows 8.3 % of rolls in the column land on the gap, so the difference
between options 1 and 2 is 5 points of loss on one roll in twelve.
The table data stays as printed, with the gap in `known_gaps`; an engine
applies this ruling on top, as it applies errata.

supersession like any ruling (see [rulings/README.md](README.md)).
