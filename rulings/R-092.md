# R-092 — Abstract truck losses: timing, base and rounding

## Problem

58.42 finds the monthly truck loss the way 32.57 finds motorisation point
losses: in the month's first naval convoy stage, for the previous month, of
the points on the map (boxes included). It then lets the owner remove
losses from anywhere, but in proportion by truck type and between convoy
and attached trucks. Three things are unclear for an engine:

- whether trucks outside the 32.57 base (off-map holding areas, trucks
  aboard ships) count;
- which naval convoy stage is meant in the Logistics sequence of play,
  whose convoy handling differs from the Land Game's;
- how to round when the proportional split leaves fractions (58.42's own
  example divides evenly).

## Options

1. **32.57 timing and base; total rounded up, split by largest remainder.**
   Consequence: the loss total is fixed first, then each type/location
   share gets its whole part and the leftover points go to the largest
   fractions. Deterministic and exact.
2. **Each share rounded separately (up).** Consequence: simple, but the
   total can exceed the chart percentage when shares are many and small.
3. **Each share rounded separately (down), with a minimum of one point in
   total.** Consequence: losses are lighter than the chart for small
   fleets.

## Decision

Option 1. The loss is taken with 32.57's timing (the month's first naval convoy stage) and base (on-map trucks, boxes included), the total rounded up and split among types and locations by largest remainder.

## Rationale

58.42 says the loss is found the same way as motorisation point losses, so
32.57 governs both timing and base: the opening naval convoy stage of each
month, and the points on the map including the Tripolitania–Tunis boxes.
The Logistics sequence still has a naval convoy stage (III) every turn, so
the first turn of the month supplies it without change; trucks aboard ships
or not yet arrived are not "on the map" and do not count. For rounding,
58.43 rounds truck cuts up, and rounding the total up matches it; splitting
by largest remainder then keeps the proportions 58.42 requires while making
the shares add to exactly that total, which rounding each share separately
(options 2 and 3) cannot promise. 58.42's own example is reproduced exactly
by this method. An engine gets a deterministic split, with the owner
choosing only on ties.
