# R-012 — A defender who commits nothing: is the three-hex retreat obligatory?

## Problem

15.29a: a defender who allocates no units to an assault has every unit in the
hex retreat three hexes and take three disorganisation points. 15.82, the
general retreat rule, lets a retreating force stop short and pay 10 % losses
per hex not retreated. Read together they invite a trick: withhold
everything, "retreat" zero hexes, and pay 30 % instead of fighting —
sometimes cheaper than an assault against a strong attacker, and it avoids
being Engaged. NJHarman:[^nj-com]

> Can only withhold all units and retreat if path exists and is taken
> [15.29]. I.e can't use [15.82] and take 10-30% losses for hexes not
> retreated.

[^nj-com]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Combat,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#combat>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged CLARIFICATION). The quoted item is the second CLARIFICATION under
  that heading. See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **The retreat must be possible and must be made in full** (NJHarman). A
   defender may allocate nothing only if a three-hex retreat path exists;
   the units then retreat the full three hexes, and the 15.82 buy-out does
   not apply. Consequence: withholding is an escape, never a way to convert
   an assault into a fixed 30 % loss.
2. **15.82 applies as written.** The units retreat as far as they can or
   choose to and pay 10 % per hex short, exactly as after a CRT retreat.
   Consequence: consistent with every other retreat; opens the buy-out above
   and lets a surrounded defender who commits nothing take 30 % rather than
   surrender.
3. **Allocating nothing is not allowed when no path exists.** As option 1
   for the path requirement, but if a path exists the defender may still
   stop short under 15.82. Consequence: closes the surrounded case only.

## Decision

Option 2. A defender who commits nothing retreats under the general retreat rule, so he may stop short and pay 10 % per hex not retreated, as after any other retreat.

## Rationale

The text supports option 2 more than the others: 15.29a says the units
"retreat three hexes", and 15.82 defines what a retreat of a stated number
of hexes means, penalties included; nothing in 15.29 excepts it. But the
result is an odd incentive, and the 3 DP in 15.29a are described as the
equivalent of a 30 % loss, which suggests the designers assumed the retreat
would actually happen and the DP were the whole price. Option 1 is the
cleaner game; option 2 is the printed rule. The restated text presently
said only that withheld strength counts for nothing (SPI 15.29, 15.8) and
did not resolve this.

We take the printed rule. Our policy departs from the text only for a real
exploit, and the engine probe
([R-012.json](https://github.com/basmith7/cna-engine/blob/15a12384f3bd7aca3f815ad4f39ef8d5a52ecca2/probes/R-012.json))
finds none: counting table losses, fighting costs the defender less than
the 30 % buy-out on average in every column, the closest being +17
(29.4 % against 30 %). What the buy-out offers at the top columns is
certainty and escaping Engaged, not a lower expected loss. Option 2 also
keeps one retreat procedure for every cause, which is simplest for an
engine. The restated rule now says so
([Combat — close assault](../rules/60-combat.md)).

Decided under Brian's delegation of 2026-09-26; open to dispute and
supersession like any ruling ([rulings/README.md](README.md)).
