# R-166 — Which hexes Operation Crusader's pipeline fills

## Problem

62.5 runs a water pipeline from the railhead at C3430 to Bir Khamsa (C3029)
"in a straight line". Pipeline is laid hex by hex (52.24), so the set-up
needs a hex list. The two hexes are four apart, but not on one of the six
hex directions: a line drawn between their centres crosses row 32 exactly
on the edge between C3229 and C3230. Rows 33 and 31 are clear (C3330 and
C3130); the middle hex is not.

## Options

1. **Through C3229.** The pipeline fills C3330, C3229, C3130 and C3029,
   following the track printed from the railhead to Bir Khamsa.
2. **Through C3230.** The pipeline fills C3330, C3230, C3130 and C3029.
3. **Both middle hexes.** Five hexes, C3229 and C3230 side by side.

## Decision

Option 1. Operation Crusader starts with pipeline in C3330, C3229, C3130 and
C3029 (`construction.pipeline_hexes_built`). C3430 is not listed: it is a
working railroad hex, which already counts as Commonwealth pipeline under
52.22.

## Rationale

Both one-hex choices are four hexes long, the shortest a pipeline between
the two hexes can be, so "straight" alone cannot pick one. The map can: a
track runs from C3430 through C3330, C3229 and C3130 into C3029, and
laying the pipe beside the track the engineers would use is the natural
reading of a line drawn between the railhead and the bir. Option 2 has no
such anchor, and option 3 adds a hex the text does not need; one hex more
of pipeline is a free water source in the desert, which matters in a
scenario that turns on supplying the advance.

Decided under Brian's delegation of 2026-09-26; open to dispute and
supersession like any ruling (see [rulings/README.md](README.md)).
