# R-004 — Terrain cost for a formation that is part motorised, part on foot

## Problem

The Terrain Effects Chart (8.37) gives separate hex and hexside costs for
motorised and non-motorised units, but a parent formation moving as one
counter can contain both: an infantry division with an attached tank
battalion, or a foot brigade whose first-line trucks carry some of its
points. The glossary's definition of a motorised unit (3.1) speaks of the
unit's movement being provided by vehicles, and 8.91–8.92 say a unit is
motorised when it has enough vehicles to carry *all* of itself; neither
says how to price the movement of a mixed stack that moves as one.
NJHarman:[^nj-mov]

> A Formation with both non-motorized and motorized (even just 1st line
> attached trucks) units must pay the most expensive CP cost for each hex
> and hexside.
>
> *Motorized vs non-motorized is confused in application to Terrain Effects
> Chart. [3.1] says "motorized unit" is a unit whose movement is produced by
> wheeled or tracked vehicles. Do we ignore 1st line trucks? What about
> infantry Div with attached tanks. If any vehicle makes unit motorized,
> then what unit doesn't have some trucks attached?*

[^nj-mov]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Movement,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#movement>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged INTERPRETATION). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`. The quoted item is the
  second INTERPRETATION under that heading.

## Options

1. **Dearest column wins** (NJHarman). A formation containing any
   non-motorised element and any vehicle pays, for each hex and hexside, the
   higher of the two chart costs, and is bound by both columns' prohibitions.
   Consequence: a foot division cannot be made cheap by attaching a tank
   battalion, nor a tank regiment made able to cross an escarpment by
   attaching infantry; the stack moves at the pace of its slowest and
   least agile part.
2. **The parent's own status governs.** A formation is motorised if, and
   only if, the parent counter has enough vehicle points for every TOE point
   it carries (8.92); attached vehicles that do not carry the foot elements
   do not change that. Consequence: the chart is read once per counter, but
   a motorised element inside a foot formation pays foot costs and could be
   marched where vehicles are prohibited.
3. **Each element pays its own column.** The CP charge to the parent is the
   sum or maximum of what its elements would pay separately, and any
   element's prohibition stops the stack. Consequence: the same result as
   option 1 for prohibitions, but requires the parent's CP ledger to be
   split by element, which nothing else in the CP system does.

## Decision

None yet — proposed.

## Rationale

The printed rules already carry the principle that a parent moves at the
rate of its slowest attached unit: an HQ's capability point allowance is that
of the slowest unit attached to it ([Units and state —
Headquarters](../rules/10-units-and-state.md#headquarters)), and a unit is
motorised only when *all* of it can ride (8.91–8.92, restated in [Movement —
Motorised units and trucks](../rules/40-movement.md#trucks)). Option 1 is
the direct extension of that principle to the terrain chart and needs no new
bookkeeping. Option 2 follows the letter of 8.92 but lets vehicles enter
terrain the chart forbids them; option 3 reaches the same answer as option 1
by a route the CP system does not otherwise support.
