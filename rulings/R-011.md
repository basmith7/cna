# R-011 — Whose raw points form the loss base in 15.83b

## Problem

15.83 turns a percentage result into TOE losses by multiplying it against a
total of raw close-assault points. Step (b) tells the affected player to
total the points "involved in the assault" and then says what the defender
adds; it does not say in so many words that each player totals *his own*
points rather than everyone's. SPI's extended combat example has each side
total its own. NJHarman:[^nj-com]

> 15.83b says […] The combat example has each side totaling their Close Assault
> points. Each side should total only their Close Assault points.
> *Interesting effect to represent stronger force, inflicting more
> casualties. But combat differential and overrun already is doing this.*

[^nj-com]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Combat,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#combat>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged CLARIFICATION). The elision is his quotation of the printed 15.83b
  sentence, not reproduced here. See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **Each side totals its own raw points** (NJHarman; the printed example).
   The attacker's percentage is taken of the attacker's committed raw points,
   the defender's of the defender's (plus pinned, retreated-in and, in an
   overrun, withheld units). Consequence: a small force assaulting a large
   one loses a small number of points even on a bad roll; strength
   differences act through the differential and overrun, not the base.
2. **Both sides total all raw points in the assault.** Each percentage is
   taken of the combined total. Consequence: losses scale with the size of
   the whole engagement, so a battalion probing a division can be wiped out
   by a modest percentage; the differential would count twice.

## Decision

None yet — proposed.

## Rationale

Option 1. The sentence following (b) tells the *defender* what to add to
"this total", which only makes sense if the total is the defender's own;
15.83c then has the attacker and defender round their results differently,
which presupposes separate bases; and the example resolves it that way.
The restated procedure states option 1 explicitly ([Combat — Percentage
losses](../rules/60-combat.md#casualties)).
