# R-122 — Which withheld defenders count in the loss base?

## Problem

Two printed cases disagree about a defender's withheld strength. 15.16
lets the non-phasing player withhold individual TOE points and says that,
unlike the phasing player's, those points count in the percentage-loss
base. 15.83b, which gives the procedure itself, adds pinned and
retreated-in units to the defender's base and adds units "withheld for any
reason" only in an overrun. Our combat page followed 15.16 in one place
and 15.83 in another. cna-engine follows 15.83.

## Options

1. **15.83 governs.** Withheld defensive strength joins the base only in
   an overrun.
2. **15.16 governs.** Every TOE point the defender withholds always joins
   the base.
3. **Each case keeps its own subject.** 15.16 speaks of *points* withheld
   from a unit that otherwise fights, and 15.83 of *units* withheld whole.
   Points held back from a committed unit always count. A unit withheld
   entirely counts only in an overrun.

## Decision

Option 3. TOE points the defender holds back from a unit that commits
others always count in his loss base. A unit he withholds entirely counts
only in an overrun. Pinned, retreated-in and out-of-ammunition units always
count (15.12, 15.15).

## Rationale

Both cases are printed rules, and option 3 is the only reading under which
both hold as written. 15.16 is about splitting a unit's points: its first
sentence lets the phasing player split a unit or hold some of its points
back, and its second gives the defender the same power over "individual
TOE Strength Points". 15.83b is about units: pinned units, retreated units,
"Defensive units withheld". Read this way, the defender cannot shrink his
loss base by committing one point of a battalion and holding back the
rest, which is the abuse 15.16's "unlike Phasing units" rules out. But a
battalion he keeps out of the fight entirely stands clear of the losses
until an overrun sweeps the hex, as 15.83 and 14.21 ("defending units
withheld may be affected by Close Assault") allow. Option 1 brings back
the one-point abuse. Option 2 deletes 15.83's overrun clause, which then
has nothing to add. The reading is also consistent with
[R-011](R-011.md), which already names withheld *units* as the overrun
addition. For an engine: in the defender's base, include every point of
any unit that commits at least one point, plus pinned, retreated-in and
out-of-ammunition units, plus whole withheld units only in an overrun. The
same set may absorb losses.
