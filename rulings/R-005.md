# R-005 — Rail movement: what one "stack" per direction may carry

## Problem

Three cases describe the Commonwealth rail allowance. 8.72 allows units
*and/or* supplies, one stack in each direction, any distance. 8.74 restates
it as one unit (or stack) one way and another stack, "or perhaps supplies",
the other way. 8.77 lets units be picked up and dropped anywhere along the
line so long as stacking and direction are respected and no CP were spent.
Read together they leave open whether a single eastbound run may carry both
units and supplies, and whether a run may set some cargo down and take other
cargo on. NJHarman's reading:[^nj-mov]

> Rail Movement
>
> In each direction may rail units OR supplies.
>
> As [8.77] states units and supplies may be picked up/dropped off as long as
> total capacity is never exceeded.
>
> *Rules are written way more complicated than needs be. [8.72] and [8.74]
> say largely same thing and conflict with [8.77].*

[^nj-mov]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Movement,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#movement>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged CLARIFICATION). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **Units or supplies, not both, per direction; pick-ups and drops allowed
   within capacity** (NJHarman). Consequence: two trains a stage, each of a
   single kind of cargo; the 2-SP and supply limits are checked at every
   point of the run, so a train may drop a battalion at Matruh and pick up
   another for Alexandria.
2. **Units and/or supplies in one stack per direction, within both limits at
   once; pick-ups and drops allowed.** Consequence: a single run may carry a
   battalion and a supply unit together, as long as the stack never exceeds
   2 SP of units or the §32 supply limit at any point; otherwise as
   option 1.
3. **One fixed load per direction, no intermediate pick-ups or drops.**
   Consequence: 8.77 is read as describing where a run may *start and end*,
   not as allowing a changing load; the simplest to police, but it makes the
   sentence about picking up and dropping off along the line redundant.

## Decision

Option 2. Each direction's single rail stack may mix units and supplies and may change its load along the line, provided the unit and supply limits hold at every point.

## Rationale

8.72 is the general statement and says units *and/or* supplies; 8.74 is an
illustration of the one-run-each-way limit, and its "or perhaps supplies"
is an example, not an exclusion. 8.77 then says loads may change along the
line, subject to the limits. The restated rule follows option 2 ([Movement —
Rail movement](../rules/40-movement.md#rail-movement)): a stack is units
and/or supplies, one run each way, loads changeable, both limits checked
throughout. Option 1 is a legitimate simplification but has to read the
"and/or" of 8.72 as "or"; option 3 discards 8.77.
