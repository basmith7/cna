# R-008 — "In combat or Engaged" in 8.53d, and the break-off sentence of 8.52

## Problem

8.52 closes by exempting a reacting unit from the break-off cost of 8.24.
8.53d bars reaction by any unit that is "in combat" or Engaged, and 8.53c
already bars any unit inside an enemy zone of control. If the
only way to be liable for a break-off cost is to be in Contact (which needs
an enemy ZOC) or Engaged, and both of those states bar reaction, the 8.52
exemption has nothing to apply to. NJHarman:[^nj-words]

> [8.52] Last sentence is confusing/pointless as heck. It says Reacting unit
> […]
>
> *[8.53.d] says units that are in combat or Engaged may not React at all. No
> idea what "in combat" means. Probably meant in Contact. But could only be
> in Contact if under EZOC and [8.53.c] says units under EZOC may not React.
> Rule repetition leads to contradictions and ambiguity.*

[^nj-words]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § More Words, Is
  Bad Words, <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#more-words-is-bad-words>,
  CC-BY-SA 4.0 (tagged CORRECTION). The elision is his quotation of the
  printed 8.52 sentence, which is not reproduced here. See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **"In combat" means "in Contact"; 8.52's sentence is dead text.** A unit
   in an enemy ZOC, in Contact or Engaged never reacts, and the exemption in
   8.52 is a leftover that changes nothing. Consequence: the restated rule
   can drop the sentence and lose nothing.
2. **"In combat" means currently the target of a declared barrage or close
   assault this segment.** A unit that has been fired on or assaulted in the
   current combat segment may not react to a later move in the same cycle;
   8.52's exemption covers the corner case of a unit whose Contact marker
   is stale — placed at the start of the segment, its enemy has since moved
   away — reacting to a different enemy without paying to break a contact
   that no longer exists. Consequence: both sentences do work, at the price
   of tracking who was attacked this segment.
3. **"In combat" is meaningless and the phrase is struck**, leaving only
   Engaged in 8.53d. Consequence: same play as option 1 but by deletion
   rather than reading.

## Decision

Option 1. "In combat" in 8.53d means in Contact, so a unit in an enemy ZOC, in Contact or Engaged never reacts, and the break-off exemption in 8.52 has no case to apply to.

## Rationale

Option 1. None of the printed cases defines an "in combat" state, while Contact is a defined marker state that sits naturally beside Engaged; reading the phrase as Contact is the smallest step from the text. Option 2 does give 8.52's last sentence work to do, but only by inventing a new state ("attacked this segment") that the rules never track, and it leans on the still-proposed R-003 for the stale-marker corner case; the ruling's own analysis notes the two readings almost never differ in play, since a unit attacked this segment is nearly always in the attacker's ZOC. Option 1 is the simpler thing for an engine to check — the existing ZOC, Contact and Engaged states — and the restated text already says reaction never pays the break-off fee, which is harmless under this reading. Option 3 reaches the same play by striking printed words, which this project avoids when a reading will do. The restated rule now names Contact explicitly ([Movement — Reaction](../rules/40-movement.md#reaction)).
