# R-031 — When the land-support air phase is taken

## Problem

In the Logistics sequence (48.0) the land-support air phase (F) sits between the Commonwealth fleet phase (E) and reserve designation (G). The Land Game outline treats the phases up to the fleet as joint and those from reserve designation on as done by Player A, then Player B. Phase F names no player, so it is unclear whether it is flown once per stage by both sides together, or twice, once in each player's half.

## Options

1. **Once per stage, joint.** Both sides fly land support together after the fleet phase and before Player A's reserve designation. Consequence: Player B's missions happen before Player A moves, so air cannot support B's own later attacks in the same stage.
2. **Per player, before reserves.** Player A flies phase F, then does G–L; Player B then does F–L. Consequence: each side's air support lines up with its own movement and combat; the opponent may still scramble and intercept.
3. **Defer to the Air Game rules.** Treat 48.0 as silent and read the Air Game sections (§33–46) for the answer. Consequence: the ruling waits until the Air Game is restated.

## Decision

Option 1. The land-support air phase is flown once per stage by both sides together, after the Commonwealth fleet phase and before Player A's reserve designation.

## Rationale

The printed phase F (48.0) is written for both sides at once: its
tactical-maintenance segment lets both players ready aircraft, and its
air-combat segment resolves scrambles and interceptions between opposing
planes in the same pass. The first phase explicitly handed to Player A is
reserve designation (G), so F falls in the joint block before it. Option 2
would add a second pass the text does not print, and option 3 is not needed
because 48.0 already gives the answer. The cost noted under option 1 —
Player B's air support comes before Player A moves — is the designer's
sequence, not an exploit, and applies to both sides alike since the A/B roles
are chosen each stage. An engine needs only one joint air phase per stage.
