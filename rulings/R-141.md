# R-141 — Which organisation-chart components count toward a shell

## Problem

9.26 judges a division a shell by how many of its assignable
"brigade-equivalents" are attached, and a brigade by how many of its
assignable battalions ("combat units") are. The Formation Organisation Charts (19.3) list
everything a formation may hold, mixing brigades and regiments with
battalions, artillery, companies of 0 SP and slots printed with no figure
at all (the Commonwealth infantry division's anti-tank unit, the 3 SP
brigade's recce and "any unit" slots). Nothing says which of them make up
the denominator. cna-engine asked, per component.

## Options

1. **Everything on the row.** Every component counts, whatever its size.
   A division would then be a shell unless most of its battalions were
   attached too, though 9.26 tells us to judge it by brigades.
2. **By size, one step below the parent.** A division (5 SP) counts its
   components of 2 SP or more, each as one brigade-equivalent; a brigade or
   regiment (2 or 3 SP) counts its components of 1 SP or more, each as one
   battalion. A component of 0 SP, or one the chart prints with no figure,
   never counts.
3. **By kind.** Count only infantry, tank and cavalry units, leaving out
   artillery, anti-tank, anti-aircraft, recce and engineers.

## Decision

Option 2. A division counts its components of 2 SP or more, a brigade or
regiment its components of 1 SP or more, and a component of 0 SP or with no
printed figure never counts; each component carries the answer as
`counts_for_shell` in `data/tables/formation-organisation.json`.

## Rationale

9.26 names the unit of count by size: brigade-equivalents for a division,
battalions for a brigade, and its example tells us to ignore battalions
when judging the division. The stacking points the chart prints beside each
symbol are the game's own measure of size (9.4: a brigade is 2 SP, a
battalion 1, a company 0), so they give the cut without any further
reading. "Combat units" covers artillery, anti-tank and recce battalions as
much as infantry (they all have combat ratings), so option 3 adds a
distinction the case does not make. A slot printed without a figure can be
filled by a 0 SP company (the anti-tank unit, the "any unit" slot) or is
optional, so counting it would make a formation a shell for lacking a piece
it need never have.

Results worth knowing, for an engine:

- A Commonwealth infantry division counts its three brigades; an armoured
  division its brigades and support group (three, then two from turn 71).
- A Commonwealth 3 SP brigade counts five: three infantry battalions, the
  artillery unit and the light anti-aircraft or machine-gun battalion.
- The 5th Light Panzer Division has one brigade-equivalent, its armoured
  regiment: it is a shell exactly when that regiment is not attached, or is
  attached as a shell (9.27). Its battalions do not count.
- The German 15th Infantry Brigade is built of three regiments, and counts
  them as its three.
- A 2 SP artillery group (15th Panzer, 164th Leichte) counts as one
  brigade-equivalent of its division, as its printed figure says.
- An `any_of` slot counts once, whichever unit fills it.
- The Commonwealth armoured brigade type II, which applies in two separate
  periods, is now two rows with one `turns` each.
