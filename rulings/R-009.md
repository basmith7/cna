# R-009 — Do terrain shifts on the Barrage Table protect the phasing player's units too?

## Problem

12.33 says that a target standing in certain terrain has the barrage
against it resolved a number of rows lower, "benefitting the defender", and
gives a defensive example. Barrage is not one-directional: in the same
combat segment the non-phasing player fires back at the phasing player's
hexes. If "defender" in 12.33 means the non-phasing player, a phasing
battalion in a fortification gets no protection from the reply; if it means
"whoever is being shelled", both sides get the shift. NJHarman:[^nj-bar]

> [12.33] It is unclear if terrain adjustments to Barrage are intended to
> apply to attacker. I rule that they do.
>
> *In part because that is how every other war game I'm aware of does it. In
> part because [14.0] specifically says […]. In other words, attacker /
> defender are not synonyms for
> phasing / non-phasing. So, when Barrage rules mention "defender" it means
> "the side undergoing Barrage" regardless of which is the Phasing or
> "attacking" side.*

[^nj-bar]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Barrage,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#barrage>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged INTERPRETATION). The elision is his paraphrase of the printed 14.0
  sentence about which player is attacker or defender, not reproduced here.
  See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **"Defender" means the target of the barrage, either side** (NJHarman).
   Every barrage is shifted by the target hex's terrain. Consequence:
   symmetrical, and the same rule an engine applies to anti-armour fire
   under 14.0's explicit statement that either player may be attacker or
   defender.
2. **"Defender" means the non-phasing player.** Only barrages fired by the
   phasing player are shifted; the reply is resolved at full strength
   whatever terrain the phasing units occupy. Consequence: a unit that has
   just moved into a fortified hex and stopped gets no benefit from it until
   the enemy's half; the terrain chart's barrage column would have to be
   read as applying to one side only, which nothing on it says.

## Decision

Option 1. The terrain shift protects whichever side's hex is being barraged, phasing or not.

## Rationale

Option 1. The Terrain Effects Chart lists a barrage shift per terrain type
without qualification, and 12.33 explains the shift as protection for
whoever is in that terrain; its example happens to use a towed gun as the
target and does not say which player owns it. The one place the printed
rules do distinguish attacker from phasing player — 14.0 — says the roles
are independent of who is phasing, and there is no reason to read the
barrage rules, written for the same combat segment, differently. The
restated rule applies the shift to every target ([Combat —
Barrage: targets and terrain](../rules/60-combat.md)), and now says so
explicitly.

The engine probe
([R-009.json](https://github.com/basmith7/cna-engine/blob/15a12384f3bd7aca3f815ad4f39ef8d5a52ecca2/probes/R-009.json))
shows the stakes: against phasing infantry in a level-two fortification,
option 2 would make the reply barrage pin or destroy up to 39 percentage
points more often (39 % against 0 % at 3-4 points) — the fortification
would be worth nothing to the side that just occupied it. One symmetric
rule is also simpler for an engine.
