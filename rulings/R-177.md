# R-177 — What a halftrack unit is in combat

## Problem

14.48 treats "halftracks" (some motorised Axis infantry) specially under
anti-armour fire: when they are the only points in the hex that the fire can
touch, their armour protection doubles; losing one takes its carrying truck
points with it; and errata E-004 caps such losses at two TOE points a
segment. The characteristics charts name the units: German `j` (the `‡` on
its CPA) and Italian `t` count as halftracks only while carried by truck or
motorisation points, and otherwise have no armour protection. Both rows
print an armour protection of 1 with no condition attached.

Nothing else in the rules says what a halftrack is. The barrage table splits
targets into gun, armour and infantry classes; the assault procedure lets
only unpinned armour- and gun-class units be withheld; and §32 replaces
trucks with motorisation points without saying what a halftrack loss
removes. The glossary's armoured vehicle excludes halftracks.

## Options

1. **Infantry that carries armour while motorised.** A halftrack unit is
   infantry class for every purpose (barrage column, pins, the barrage
   footnote on infantry in trucks, close assault, and no withholding). Only
   while it is wholly carried by truck or motorisation points does it have
   its printed armour protection, which makes it a target of anti-armour
   fire under 14.48. Each halftrack TOE point lost to anti-armour fire
   removes the trucks that carried it: one motorisation point under §32; in
   the full game, the owner's choice of the unit's truck points with at
   least one infantry TOE point of capacity between them (one medium, one
   heavy or two light, per the Truck Characteristics Chart). The two-point
   cap applies in every anti-armour segment. Consequence: one class per
   unit, no new barrage column, and the halftrack rules touch only
   anti-armour fire, where 14.48 puts them.
2. **Armour class while motorised.** As option 1, but a motorised halftrack
   unit uses the armour column of the barrage table and may be withheld
   like armour. Consequence: a unit changes barrage class as it mounts and
   dismounts, and an infantry battalion can be withheld from close assault,
   which no case grants infantry.
3. **Armour class always.** Treat `j` and `t` as armour whatever their
   transport. Consequence: contradicts the chart notes, which give the unit
   no armour protection on foot.

## Decision

Option 1. A halftrack unit (`halftrack: true` on its characteristics row)
is infantry class throughout and may never be withheld; its printed armour
protection counts only while it is wholly carried by truck or motorisation
points, and each of its TOE points lost to anti-armour fire takes one
motorisation point, or in the full game truck points carrying at least one
infantry TOE point (owner's choice), with it; no more than two such points
are lost in any anti-armour segment.

## Rationale

The printed text supports it. The glossary excludes halftracks from
armoured vehicles; the charts call `j` and `t` infantry battalions; and
14.48 itself describes halftracks as motorised leg infantry, so the barrage
column and the withholding rule (armour and guns only) read them as
infantry. The chart notes make the armour protection conditional on
carriage, and a unit is carried only when all its TOE points are in trucks
(21.43), so a partly carried unit has none. 14.48 removes the truck
points that carried the lost point along with it; under §32 that
is one motorisation point, as [R-123](R-123.md) assigns one per TOE point. In the full
game a heavy truck point carries two infantry points, so the loss is
rounded up to a whole truck point rather than leaving fractions; the
owner picks which, as he picks the absorbing TOE points. E-004 applies the
two-point cap whatever the situation, so it is not limited to
the halftracks-only hex in which protection doubles.
