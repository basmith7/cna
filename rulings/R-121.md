# R-121 — Abstract supply: ammunition for company-equivalents

## Problem

32.21 charges abstract ammunition "by the number of individual
battalion-equivalents": 1 point for a non-phasing battalion-equivalent to
take part in an assault, 2 to barrage, and double for a phasing unit. It
says nothing about company-equivalents, which include every battalion shell
(9.28). For fuel, 32.24 charges a tank company-equivalent half a tank
battalion's cost and exempts other company-equivalents. Does a company pay
a fraction, one point, or nothing? cna-engine charges nothing.

## Options

1. **Nothing.** The rule counts battalion-equivalents only; companies fight
   for free, as most of them move for free under 32.24.
2. **As a battalion-equivalent.** Every combat counter pays the full rate.
3. **Half.** Two company-equivalents count as one battalion-equivalent, as
   20.15 does. A side's company-equivalents in one combat pay together, and
   a half point left over rounds up.

## Decision

Option 3. A company-equivalent pays half a battalion-equivalent's
ammunition. A side's company-equivalents in one barrage or assault pay
together, and a half point left over is rounded up.

## Rationale

32.21 sets the unit of account as the battalion-equivalent and says a
larger formation pays by its parts. Read the same way, a smaller unit pays
as the part of a battalion it is, and the rules already put that part at
one half: 20.15 trades one battalion-equivalent for two company-equivalents,
and 32.24 charges a tank company half a tank battalion's fuel. 32.24's
exemption for other companies is a fuel rule, and it rests on a fact
about vehicles: most companies have no engines to feed. Every unit that
fights fires its weapons. Option 1 would let any company, and any battalion
reduced to a shell, barrage and assault for free, which rewards running
units down to shells. Option 2 charges a company as much as a full
battalion, which no printed figure supports. Only a non-phasing company in
an assault (half of 1) produces a fraction, so the rounding rule is
needed only there. For an engine: count a side's company-equivalents in the
combat, charge `ceil(n / 2)` times the battalion rate, and add the
battalion-equivalents' cost as usual.
