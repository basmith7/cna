# R-168 — How far an off-map air base is from a map hex

## Problem

Planes fly from places the map cannot measure: the Tripolitania, Tripoli,
Gabes and Tunis boxes, the Sicily, Italy and Crete boxes, Malta (an inset
out of scale and out of place, 44.11) and the Commonwealth's bases beyond
map E. The rules give three kinds of figure and no method for joining them
to an arbitrary hex:

- 37.4's Air Distance Chart gives hexes between pairs of named points: the
  four western boxes, Malta, Nofilia and Benghazi (part A); Crete, Malta,
  Sicily and Italy against Malta and seven coastal towns from Nofilia to
  Alexandria (part B). Some part B cells are **P**, which the chart explains
  only as "no direct flight between the two points".
- 36.53's chart gives each Commonwealth base an entry hex on map E's edge
  and the hexes from the base to it. 37.4's second worked example adds the
  base's figure to the hexes counted on the map to that entry hex.
- 44.11 says Malta's distances to Africa are in a box on the Malta inset.
  The printed Map A inset has no such box (jp2 187 shows only the Italy and
  Sicily holding boxes).

An engine ranging a mission from Sicily to a hex south of Benghazi, or from
Malta to a hex beside Tobruk, needs one rule for all of these, and needs to
know whether a P stops only a flight to the named town or to the country
around it.

## Options

1. **Chart pairs only.** An off-map base may fly only to the places the
   charts name. Most of the map is unreachable from off-map bases, though
   37.4 offers the chart to save counting, not to limit where planes go.
2. **Shortest path through charted map places; P removes only its figure.**
   Range from an off-map base to a hex is its chart figure to a charted map
   place plus the hexes on from there, the shortest such path. A P pair simply has no figure. Sicily then
   reaches Tobruk at Derna's 105 plus 21 on the map, so P forbids nothing.
3. **Shortest path, and P closes the country around the place.** As 2, but
   an off-map area may not fly to any map hex that is strictly nearer, on the
   map, to a place the chart marks P for that area than to every place it
   gives a figure for. Ties stay open.
4. **Shortest path, and P closes only the place's own hex.** As 2, but only
   the hex of the P place is barred; the hex beside it is open.

## Decision

Option 3. A flight from an off-map point (a box, Malta, a Commonwealth
base) to a map hex takes one chart figure to a charted map place, or to a
base's entry hex, and counts the rest on the map (`map_geom.distance`,
across seams); its distance is the shortest such path. The path never runs
through a second off-map point: Tripoli to Benghazi is the printed 88, not
Tripoli to the Tripolitania box (32) and on to Benghazi (44). Between two
off-map points the listed figure is the distance; a pair the chart does not
list has no direct flight, except that two points each with a figure to the
map may be joined across it (Crete to Ismailia: Alexandria's 84, the map to
E3433, then 9). Every Malta inset hex is the point Malta, and 37.4's Malta
figures stand for 44.11's missing Malta box. A P removes that figure and
also bars the area's planes from every map hex strictly nearer the P place
than every place the area has a figure for.

## Rationale

The printed text supports the shortest path directly. 37.11 lets a plane
fly any path the player likes, and 37.4 calls the chart a shortcut for
counting hexes, so a route through a charted point (Malta to Tobruk, then
16 hexes on to Bardia: 142) is as good as the chart's own figure (Malta to
Bardia: 144). This is the only place the chart is longer than such a path.
A box is an area, not a point, so a chain through one understates: the
Tripolitania box is 32 from Tripoli and 44 from Benghazi, yet the chart puts
Tripoli 88 from Benghazi. Only map hexes are points the path may pass.
37.4's worked example 2 (Agedabia to Ismailia: 140 hexes on the map to
E3433 plus 9 off it, 149) is this rule for the Commonwealth bases, and
convoy-lanes.json's rally points (41.62, 42.52) already range North African
bases to the convoy lanes the same way.

P has to mean more than a missing number. The chart calls it prohibited,
and the P cells form regions: Sicily and Italy reach only Benghazi and
Derna, Malta nothing east of Bardia, Crete everything but the western
Gulf of Sidra. Under option 2 any of those places is still reached by a chain
through its neighbour, so P would forbid nothing. Option 4 bars one hex and
leaves the hex beside it open. Option 3 keeps the regions the chart draws,
is computed once per hex, and needs no figure the chart does not print.
The chart's P is about where an area's planes may operate, not about range,
so the bar applies whatever the plane's range.
