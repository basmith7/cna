# R-015 — Anti-armour terrain shifts: which side's fire do they weaken?

## Problem

Anti-armour fire is simultaneous and mutual: the phasing side fires into the
hex it is assaulting, the non-phasing side fires out of it. 14.32 says
terrain in the hex where the defending armour stands shifts the column in
the defender's favour, weakening the *attacker's* fire, and points to the
Terrain Effects Chart; the chart also lists hexside effects, and the
Anti-Armor Table itself carries an entry that applies to the attacker only.
14.0 says either player may be the attacker or defender. What is not said is
whether the non-phasing player's fire at armour that has just come up to his
hex is also shifted by *its* terrain, and whether hexside shifts apply to
both. NJHarman:[^nj-aa]

> [14.32] Anti-Armor shifts due to defender's terrain apply to both sides.
> Shifts for hexsides apply only to attacker. Attacking through slope into
> mountain would be 3L shifts to attacker's and 2L shifts to defender's
> anti-armor fire.
>
> *[14.0] says either player may be the attacker or defender. [14.6] chart
> has entry applying only to attacker. [11.4] chart says "Terrain may benefit
> either Player". Also says "Non-phasing is benefited by enemy forces having
> to enter their hex. Meaning defenders don't incur shifts for hexsides.*

[^nj-aa]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Anti-Armor (in
  *Opinionated Additions and Changes*),
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#anti-armor>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged INTERPRETATION). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Options

1. **In-hex terrain shifts fire against armour in that hex, both sides;
   hexside shifts weaken the phasing side's fire only** (NJHarman). Fire at
   the assaulting armour (standing in its own hex) is shifted by *that*
   hex's terrain; fire at the defending armour by the defended hex's
   terrain plus the hexside crossed. Consequence: symmetrical in-hex
   protection, and the hexside asymmetry follows the chart's own note.
2. **Only the defended hex shifts anything, and only the phasing side's
   fire** (14.32 literally). The non-phasing side fires at full strength
   whatever terrain the attacking armour sits in. Consequence: simplest;
   armour attacking out of rough ground gets no cover from it.
3. **Both hex and hexside shifts apply to both sides.** Consequence: fully
   symmetrical, but contradicts the attacker-only entry on the table and
   the chart's reasoning that the hexside penalty represents having to cross
   it.

## Decision

None yet — proposed.

## Rationale

The restated rule follows the literal 14.32 (option 2) for the in-hex
shift, with the hexside shift added only when "the phasing points assault
across it" ([Combat — Terrain and anti-armour
fire](../rules/60-combat.md#terrain-and-anti-armour)). Option 1 is a
reasonable extension by analogy with R-009, and the chart's remark that
terrain may benefit either player supports it; but 14.32 is written about
the defending armour's hex and does not mention the attacker's, so
option 2 is what the text says and option 1 is what the chart implies.
The difference matters only when armour assaults out of protective terrain,
which is uncommon.
