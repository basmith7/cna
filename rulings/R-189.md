# R-189 — A set-up stack over the stacking ceiling

## Problem

9.14 limits the stacking points in a hex by its terrain and checks the
limit at the end of every movement segment. Nothing in §9 says what
happens to a stack the set-up itself leaves over that limit. cna-engine
met two such hexes in its scenarios (Graziani's Offensive's Cairo, and
A1716 in The Desert Fox) and asked whether such a stack is legal, must be
fixed at once, or binds only once something moves in. Its current build
tolerates the stack until pieces moved into the hex raise its stacking
points.

Two printed rules bear on it. The scenario introduction lets an owner
divide a group listed for several hexes as he likes, provided no hex ends
up over its stacking limit (59.2). And 59.65 settles the case for supply
units only: when the set-up's instructions force more of them into a hex
than stacking allows they may stay without penalty, no further supply unit
may be added there, and once the hex is back within its limit the normal
limits hold from then on.

## Options

1. **Illegal; fix at once.** The owner must move or reorganise the excess
   before play. Contradicts a printed set-up that names one hex, and there
   is no movement segment before play in which to fix it.
2. **Binds only at the first movement segment.** The stack is legal at
   set-up but must be within the ceiling at the end of its owner's first
   movement segment. Forces units the set-up placed to move whether or not
   their owner wants to, and a hex nobody can empty in one segment has no
   legal end state.
3. **Read 59.65 across to every unit.** Where the owner chooses the hexes,
   he must choose within the ceiling. Where the printed set-up leaves no
   choice, the stack is legal as placed, with no penalty; it may lose
   stacking points but never gain them, and once it is at or under the
   ceiling the normal limit holds from then on.

## Decision

Option 3. An owner who chooses hexes stays within the ceiling; a stack the
printed set-up forces over it is legal as placed, may not grow, and comes
under the normal limit once it falls within it.

## Rationale

Option 3 is what the printed text supports. 59.2 already binds every
owner-chosen division of a group by the ceiling, and 59.65 gives the only
printed answer for a forced overfill, for supply units: tolerate it, add
nothing, normal limits once it complies. The same scenario introduction
treats a formation over its organisation limits the same way: it operates
as it is, takes on nothing new, and once a unit leaves it may not take on
another that would break the limits. Option 3 reads the same pattern
across to stacking; options 1 and 2 would make some printed set-ups
unplayable as printed.

For an engine, 9.14 stays a check at the end of each movement segment.
Each overfilled hex carries an allowance, its stacking points at set-up.
At each check the hex passes if its stacking points are within the
ceiling, or within the allowance; then the allowance falls to the hex's
current stacking points, and is dropped for good once they are within the
ceiling. So Rommel or an empty HQ (0 SP) may end a move there, a battalion
may not, and a battalion that left in an earlier segment cannot be replaced
by another. This is slightly stricter than the engine's current build,
which compares only with the stack before this segment's movers arrived:
"nothing added" in 59.65 means the stack never regains what it lost.

Both of the engine's examples are owner choices, not forced overfills, so
under the first half of the decision they should not arise. In
`data/scenarios/grazianis-offensive.json` the Commonwealth units listed for
Cairo are placed by `place: cairo`, which means all five Cairo hexes
(E1730, E1829, E1830, E1930, E1931; `data/README.md`), and the 6th
Australian Division may go to Cairo or Helwan. In Rommel's Arrival, which
The Desert Fox extends, A1716 appears only in hex lists the Axis owner
divides "within stacking limits" (the supply units and unattached
motorisation points along the coast road); no unit is placed in A1716 by
name. An engine that puts everything in the first hex of such a list
creates the overfill itself; it should spread the group, and a set-up that
cannot be spread within the ceiling is the only case the second half of
the decision covers.
