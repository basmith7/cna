# R-013 — Do pinned units spend ammunition in a close assault?

## Problem

A pinned unit cannot be committed to a close assault; it adds no strength,
but its raw points are added to the defender's loss base (15.83b) so it
shares the casualties. Ammunition is spent by units that *fight*: the
Land Game's abstract supply rules (32.33) charge each unit that takes part
in a close assault. Whether a pinned unit — present, hit, but not fighting —
is "taking part" is not stated. NJHarman:[^nj-com]

> Pinned units do not expend ammo for Close Assault. *Couldn't find
> definitive answer. [50.12] strongly implies no. Pinned units aren't counted
> for combat strength, only for combat losses.*

[^nj-com]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Combat,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#combat>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged INTERPRETATION). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

His Discord-game notes ask the same question and put the answer as a
leaning towards no ammunition, on the same ground: pinned units count for
losses, not for strength.[^nj-discord]

[^nj-discord]: NJHarman, private notes from the Discord game, 2026-09
  (paraphrased; not part of his published house rules and not under the
  CC-BY-SA grant, so not quoted).

## Options

1. **No ammunition** (NJHarman). Only units whose points were committed to
   the assault spend ammunition; pinned, retreated-in and withheld units are
   in the loss base only. Consequence: the ammunition charge follows the
   strength contribution, which is how the restated rule reads.
2. **Every unit in the assaulted hex spends ammunition.** Presence in the
   hex is participation. Consequence: pinning becomes doubly punishing —
   the unit is shot at and pays to be shot at — and a hex of pinned units
   could run dry without ever firing.

## Decision

None yet — proposed.

## Rationale

Option 1. 15.12 and 15.83b treat pinned units as a special addition to the
loss base precisely because they are *not* participants; the ammunition
rules charge participants. The one place where a pinned unit does something
in an assault is absorbing losses, and absorbing losses costs nothing else
in the game. The Logistics Game case NJHarman cites (50.12) is outside this
edition, but the §32 abstraction restates the same charge in the same terms
([§32](../rules/95-abstract-logistics-and-air.md)).
