# R-072 — How Axis rolling stock adds up and when Axis trains move

## Problem

Case 54.43: each lot of 250 stores and 100 fuel the Axis delivers to a
controlled rail hex activates the controlled line "to the extent of" 300
tons in one direction per Operations Stage. 54.44 needs rolling stock worth
900 tons to move one stacking point of units. It is not said whether
several lots add up (so three lots give 900 tons), whether the capacity is
per direction or in total, or when the Axis moves: 54.43 says rail movement
happens in the Convoy Stage, while 54.46 applies the Commonwealth rules,
under which rail moves are made in the Operations Stage.

## Options

1. **Lots add up; capacity is per direction; Axis trains move when
   Commonwealth trains do.** Consequence: three lots allow 900 tons (or one
   stacking point) each way every stage; the "Convoy Stage" words are read
   as loose wording for the truck convoy movement segment.
2. **Lots add up; the total is shared by both directions; Axis trains move
   in the Naval Convoy Stage.** Consequence: Axis rail is roughly half as
   useful and runs at a different time from Commonwealth rail.
3. **One lot only ever gives 300 tons; more lots buy nothing.**
   Consequence: the Axis can never move units by rail (54.44 needs 900),
   which makes 54.44 dead text, so this reading is unlikely.

## Decision

Option 1. Rolling-stock lots add up, 300 tons each, the total is available in each direction every Operations Stage, and Axis trains move when Commonwealth trains do.

## Rationale

Case 54.44 asks for 900 tons of stock to move one stacking point of units,
which only makes sense if lots accumulate; option 3 would make 54.44 dead
text. 54.46 applies every Commonwealth rail rule to the Axis unless 54.4x
says otherwise, and the Commonwealth railway's capacity is per direction
(54.31, 54.32), so the Axis total is per direction too. For the same
reason Axis trains move at the Commonwealth time; the "Convoy Stage"
wording in 54.43 describes when the rolling stock is delivered and paid
for, not a separate rail step, and running both sides' trains in one step
is the simpler sequence for an engine. See [Trucks and dumps — Axis use of
the railway](../rules/logistics/40-trucks-and-dumps.md).
