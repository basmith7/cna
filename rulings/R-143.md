# R-143 — What a side learns of the losses it inflicts

## Problem

3.61 grants a player nothing about an enemy counter's status except what a
rule expressly grants, and the 3.62 list says what anti-armour fire and
close assault reveal (types, not numbers or which points absorbed the
damage). Neither says whether a side learns that an enemy unit *took*
losses, or *was disorganised*, as a bare fact. Combat results are read by
rolling dice in the open against printed tables (12.0's procedure, 14.6,
15.79), so the result cell is never hidden; but a cell is not a loss: anti-
armour damage points may be soaked by protection ratings, and close
assault's 3 DP (6.21, 15.29) turn on a share of TOE points committed, which
only the owner knows. cna-engine, building what a networked player is
shown, asked which reading to enforce.

## Options

1. **The roll and the cell, nothing applied.** Both sides see the dice and
   the table result (barrage "lose 2", a close-assault percentage, anti-
   armour damage points), as at a table. How the owner applies it is secret:
   whether and which TOE points were lost, which units they came from, any
   DP earned and any cohesion change. A counter removed from the map is seen
   as gone, because the map is open (3.62, Map).
2. **The bare fact as well.** As 1, and the inflicting side is told that a
   named unit lost points or was disorganised, without numbers.
3. **Nothing at all.** The defender resolves results privately and the
   attacker sees no cell. Needs an engine to hide dice that both players
   roll and read at a table.

## Decision

Option 1. Both sides see the roll and the table result; what that result
did to which unit, including any disorganisation, stays the owner's secret.

## Rationale

3.61 makes secrecy the default, and 3.62 names the unit types as the only
extra that combat reveals, expressly withholding numbers and which points
absorbed the damage. A bare "this unit lost points" or "this unit is
disorganised" is status of an opposing counter that no rule grants, so
option 2 adds a grant the text does not make. Option 3 hides what the
printed procedures put on the table: the barraging player reads the chart
in the open, and the close-assault differential is a total, which 3.6 says
is revealed. Option 1 is what a face-to-face game shows, and the engine's
current behaviour.
