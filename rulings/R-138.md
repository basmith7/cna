# R-138 — A lone Rommel in an enemy ZOC

## Problem

Section 31 lets Rommel react and retreat before assault against any unit
but an LRDG, and retreat through an enemy ZOC freely, but says nothing of
Rommel standing alone where an enemy ZOC reaches him. Read alone, 10.29
captures a strengthless non-combat piece left on its own under an enemy
ZOC during the enemy movement/combat phase, and bars a non-combat
piece from ever entering an unoccupied enemy-controlled hex voluntarily.
Taken at the instant it bites, 10.29 would capture a lone Rommel the moment
an enemy combat unit moved next to him, which is the very moment 31.0 lets
him react. cna-engine asked.

## Options

1. **Captured on contact.** 10.29 as printed, applied the instant his hex
   comes under an enemy ZOC. His reaction never gets a chance: any combat
   unit that can reach him takes him.
2. **Never captured.** A lone Rommel ignores 10.29 altogether: he may stand
   in, enter and leave enemy-controlled hexes at will, and only an LRDG
   raid can remove him.
3. **He gets his escape first; then 10.29 applies.** When an enemy combat
   unit moves next to a lone Rommel he may react, and when his hex is to be
   assaulted he may retreat before assault. If he is still alone in an
   enemy ZOC once he has had the chance, he is captured. He may never
   voluntarily move into an unoccupied enemy-controlled hex.

## Decision

Option 3. A lone Rommel in an enemy ZOC may first use the reaction and
retreat before assault that 31.0 gives him; if he is still alone in an
enemy ZOC after that, 10.29 captures him, and he may never move into an
unoccupied enemy-controlled hex on purpose.

## Rationale

Both printed rules can stand together if they are taken in order. 31.0
grants a reaction against an approaching unit, and the approach is exactly
what puts him in its ZOC, so option 1 would make that grant meaningless for
a Rommel on his own; 31.0 is the more specific rule and goes first. Option
2 reads far more into 31.0 than it says ("retreat through" an enemy ZOC,
not stand in one), and a piece that cannot be taken, which no enemy unit
may enter (8.13), could sit on a road or a pass and block it for good.
Under option 3 a careless Axis player can still lose him, but with a CPA of
60 he is almost never pinned (no unit's CPA exceeds his by 6) and escapes
unless every way out runs through an enemy ZOC (reaction may never enter
one). The LRDG raid (27.6) keeps its own role: it reaches him in any hex,
stacked or not, and needs no ZOC.

What counts, for an engine:

- **When he is checked.** In the enemy movement/combat phase, a lone
  Rommel in an enemy ZOC is captured at the first moment he cannot or does
  not escape: when an enemy combat unit's move puts him in its ZOC and he
  does not react away (or may not, because he was already in an enemy
  ZOC); at the end of the retreat-before-assault step of an assault on his
  hex, if he did not retreat; and at the start of the phase if he is
  already there. Stacked with any friendly combat unit he is never
  captured this way, and if his stack is destroyed or retreats he may
  retreat with it.
- **Moving.** On ordinary movement and on a reaction he obeys the ZOC
  rules of any non-combat unit: he may not enter an unoccupied
  enemy-controlled hex, and he pays the break-off cost to leave one. Only
  his retreats (before assault, or forced) go "through" enemy ZOC with
  impunity: no break-off cost and no bar on moving from one enemy-controlled
  hex to the next. A retreat that ends with him alone in an enemy ZOC
  still captures him.
- **What capture does.** He is removed for good, as a 12 on the Raid on
  Rommel Table does. He has no strength points, so the capture yields no
  prisoner points.
