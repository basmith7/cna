# R-007 — What a reacting unit may spend CP on

## Problem

The retreat-before-assault rules say outright (13.25) that a retreating unit
may spend CP only to move, plus blowing a supply dump. The reaction rules
(8.51–8.52) say that reaction is performed as movement and costs CP, but do
not say whether the reacting unit may also do the other things a unit can
pay for from the capability-point cost table (6.3) — poisoning a well,
demolition, building — while it is reacting. NJHarman's reading:[^nj-react]

> Can unit poison wells during Reaction? No, Reacting units may only expend
> CP for movement or blowing dumps same as RBA [13.25]"
>
> *Did not find anything specific. Retreat before assault [13.25] may only
> expend CP to move or blow dumps. Reaction rules say it is performed as
> "Movement". Not as a general CP expenditure.*

[^nj-react]: NJHarman, *CfNA House Rules & Interpretations*, § Reaction,
  <https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/#reaction>, CC-BY-SA 4.0
  (tagged INTERPRETATION). See `ATTRIBUTION.md`. The quoted item is the
  second INTERPRETATION under that heading.

The question came up again in the Discord game (can reacting units poison
wells?); his notes there reach the same answer by the same route — nothing
specific in the rules, 13.25 by analogy, reaction is movement — and record it
as a leaning rather than a settled call.[^nj-discord]

[^nj-discord]: NJHarman, private notes from the Discord game, 2026-09
  (paraphrased; not part of his published house rules and not under the
  CC-BY-SA grant, so not quoted).

## Options

1. **Reaction is movement only, with the same dump exception as 13.25**
   (NJHarman). A reacting unit may move and may blow a dump it leaves; it
   may not poison wells, demolish, build or perform any other 6.3 action
   during the enemy half. Consequence: the two non-phasing actions —
   reaction and retreat before assault — have one list of permitted
   expenditures.
2. **Any 6.3 action whose cost the unit can pay.** Consequence: a unit
   pulling back could poison the wells it abandons, which is exactly the
   moment a historical commander would do it; but it also means a unit can
   carry out engineering during the enemy's turn, which no case elsewhere
   allows to a non-phasing player, and the phasing player has no way to
   interrupt it.

## Decision

None yet — proposed.

## Rationale

Option 1 fits the text: 8.51 defines reaction as a form of movement and
8.52 only says the movement costs CP. Every other action on the 6.3 table is
placed by its own rule in a specific phase or segment of the *owning*
player's half (well poisoning and demolition in the engineering rules, for
example), and none of those rules opens itself to the non-phasing player.
13.25 shows the designers thinking about the same question for the sister
mechanism and answering it narrowly; reading reaction the same way is the
conservative course. Option 2 would need a rule the text does not contain.
