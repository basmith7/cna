# R-022 — Which units are "vehicles" for the without-water halving in 52.51?

## Problem

The effects of lack of water are stated for two classes and no more: 52.51
for *vehicles* (no movement or offensive close assault; halved raw strength
on defence) and 52.52 for *infantry*, defined there as everything that moves
without the benefit of vehicles (no voluntary excess over CPA, no offensive
close assault, halved defence, and TOE loss on later stages under 52.53).
Gun units — artillery, anti-tank and anti-aircraft — are neither: they move
with the benefit of vehicles (built-in or attached transport,
[Units and state — unit types](../rules/10-units-and-state.md)), so they are
not "infantry" under 52.52, but a gun is not a vehicle either. The rules do
not say which sentence a thirsty gun battalion falls under, or whether it
falls under neither and fights unaffected. NJHarman's published page lists
the 52.51 halving as a reminder;[^nj-com] his Discord-game notes read the
case as applying to vehicles and not to guns.[^nj-discord]

## Options

1. **Guns are not vehicles; 52.51 does not touch them** (NJHarman's
   reading). A gun unit without water fights at full strength; only its
   prime movers are affected, which the game does not model separately.
   Consequence: the narrow reading of "vehicle"; guns are the one class that
   shrugs off thirst, which is odd for crews but fits a rule that is about
   radiators and engines rather than men.
2. **Guns are vehicles for 52.51.** Anything that moves by vehicle is
   "vehicles" — the sentence is about the transport, and a gun without its
   tractor's water cannot move or be served. Consequence: towed guns may not
   move or close assault offensively and defend at half; the two cases
   between them cover every land unit, which reads like the design intent
   of a section that names only two classes.
3. **Guns follow 52.52 (infantry).** Their crews are men on foot once the
   gun is in position, and 52.52's effects (no voluntary CPA excess, no
   offensive close assault, half defence, TOE loss on later stages) are the
   harsher of the two. Consequence: a gun unit bleeds TOE points to thirst
   like an infantry battalion — but 52.52 defines its own scope as units
   moving without vehicles, which excludes guns by its own words.

## Decision

Option 2. Gun units are vehicles for 52.51: without water they may not move or close assault offensively, and halve their raw strength when close-assaulted.

## Rationale

Options 1 and 2 are both defensible; option 3 is not, because 52.52 defines
its class in a way that excludes guns. The case for option 2 is that §52.5
is plainly written to cover all land units in two sentences and does not
contemplate a third class; the case for option 1 is that "vehicles" is the
word printed, guns are a distinct unit type everywhere else in the rules
([3.1](../rules/10-units-and-state.md)), and a player at the table should not
have to infer a penalty the text does not name. The Land Game abstraction
ignores water entirely ([§32](../rules/95-abstract-logistics-and-air.md)),
so nothing in the restated Land Game prose changes with this ruling; it
matters for the Logistics Game and should be decided with that spec.

Option 2 is chosen, and the printed text settles it more directly than the options above suggest: 52.42, two cases earlier in the same section, lists artillery by name among the vehicles whose TOE points drink water ('Tank, Recce, Artillery, etc.'). The section therefore already treats gun units as vehicles for water, and it would be inconsistent for a gun battalion to pay water as a vehicle under 52.42 yet ignore the vehicle penalty under 52.51. 52.52 defines infantry as units moving without vehicles, which excludes guns, so the two cases between them cover every land unit, as the section's shape implies. Anti-tank and anti-aircraft units fall under the same 'etc.' as artillery. For an engine the test is simply the one 52.52 already gives: a unit that moves by vehicle is a vehicle for §52.5.
