# R-180 — A substitute stops being one when the unit it stands for arrives

## Problem

Case 19.45 lets a parent short of an assigned unit (one not yet in the game,
or reassigned under 19.66) attach an independent unit in its place, as long
as the substitute's own structure fits the missing unit's limits. The rules
do not say what happens when the missing unit later arrives, or returns to
its parent, while the substitute is still attached. Kept as a substitute,
it lets the parent carry more than its Formation Organisation and Maximum
Attachment rows allow. cna-engine asked before building 19.4x.

## Options

1. **The substitute becomes an ordinary attachment.** The moment the
   missing unit is in play again, its slot is back (an assigned unit
   occupies its slot wherever it is) and the substitute is an ordinary
   non-assigned attachment that must fit the parent's Maximum Attachment
   row. One that does not fit is detached in the owner's next
   reorganisation segment, at no Capability Point cost, into the parent's
   hex.
2. **The arriving unit waits.** The missing unit may not attach to its
   parent while a substitute holds its place.
3. **The substitute stands.** It stays a substitute for as long as it stays
   attached.

## Decision

Option 1. A substitute stands in only while the unit it replaces is
missing; once that unit is in play and assigned to the parent again, the
substitute counts against the parent's Maximum Attachment row, and any
excess is detached free in the owner's next reorganisation segment.

## Rationale

19.45 defines the substitute by the absence it fills: the missing unit is
one "not in the game" or reassigned away. When the unit arrives it arrives
assigned, and 19.2 says an assigned unit takes up its space in the parent's
structure even while detached, so the parent cannot hold both in one slot.
Option 3 keeps a fiction 19.45 no longer supports and lets a player
over-fill a formation by attaching substitutes before the historical units
land, an exploit. Option 2 has the same over-full parent on paper (the
assigned unit is counted wherever it is) and strands a unit away from its
own formation for no rule's sake.

Option 1 follows the closest printed precedent: when the Commonwealth
armoured formations' limits change, 19.25 has the player un-assign the
units the new limits no longer allow. The detachment is forced, not chosen,
so it costs no CP (19.25 names no cost for un-assigning either), and the
reorganisation segment is one of the two in which 19.4x lets a
non-assigned unit detach. Until then the parent may carry the excess, but nothing new
may be attached to it beyond its rows. A substitute that fits the
Maximum Attachment row simply stays, now as an ordinary attachment.

The same applies when an eliminated one- or two-point unit is recreated
under the §32 addition to 19.62 and goes back into its slot because nothing
was assigned to it meanwhile: an unassigned substitute in that slot becomes
an ordinary attachment. A substitute the player has formally assigned to a
free slot (19.21, or 19.23 after an elimination) is no longer a substitute
at all, and the returning unit comes back independent, as 19.62's addition
says.
