# R-188 — A blind barrage may betray a dummy tank formation

## Problem

Under [R-182](R-182.md) the barraged player tells the firer how many
targets of each class a hex holds, and a dummy tank formation counts as an
armour-class target (12.22, 3.22). 3.62's map entry lets the firer examine
the stack, which shows each counter's face (unit type and designation) but
not its contents. A dummy has no counter (16.42). So if the owner announces
more armour targets than the firer can account for from the faces he sees,
the surplus may be a dummy, and a "pinned" result on a target that has no
counter to take the marker says the same. 16.45 keeps a dummy's nature
hidden from a patrol "until he attacks". cna-engine asked which should
hold.

## Options

1. **Hide counter faces.** The firer sees only how many counters a stack
   holds until patrol or combat identifies them, so an announced count
   cannot be checked against faces. Withdraws 3.62's map grant for every
   stack on the map, dummy or not.
2. **Give the dummy a face.** A dummy is announced as a target only while it
   carries a real tank battalion's identity (16.44), shown as a counter face
   in its hex. Adds a counter the rules say does not exist (16.42), and
   puts on the map an identity 16.44 lets the owner give only when he
   reports a patrol.
3. **As printed.** Faces are visible, the dummy is announced as an
   armour-class target, and whatever the firer can infer from the count, or
   from a pinned result that marks no counter, he may use.

## Decision

Option 3. The firer sees counter faces as 3.62 grants, a dummy counts among
a hex's armour targets, and any inference he draws from the announced
count or the barrage result stands.

## Rationale

The printed text supports option 3 and contradicts the other two. 3.62's
map entry is one of the express grants 3.61 allows: a player may examine
any stack, so option 1 takes back a printed right everywhere to protect
one counterless unit. 16.42 says there are no dummy counters, and 16.44's
borrowed identity is something the owner says when he reports a patrol,
not something on the map, so option 2 invents both.

Nor does option 3 open an exploit. 16.45 protects a dummy against
reconnaissance only "until he attacks", and a barrage is an attack; 16.46
then kills the dummy the first time anti-armour fire finds no real armour
behind it. The rules expect combat to unmask dummies, and barrage merely
can do it a step earlier. The inference is also weaker than it looks.
Armour class covers more than tank battalions: self-propelled artillery
and tank destroyers, armoured headquarters and recce, tank recovery
squadrons (3.22), and an artillery or anti-tank unit with both kinds of
point is two targets, one of them armour (the note to 3.22). Which of
these a counter is depends on its TOE points, which 3.62 keeps secret. A
surplus armour target proves a dummy only in a stack whose faces rule all
of those out.

A dummy that is pinned takes no marker, since it has no counter, and loses
nothing by it: its CPA is 0 and it never fires or assaults (16.41).
